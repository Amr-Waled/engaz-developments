# Brand strategy and website expression

## Brand foundation

Define these in plain language before choosing visuals:

- Audience: who makes the decision and what risk they feel.
- Category: what the company actually does.
- Positioning: why this company is a credible choice in that category.
- Promise: the customer outcome the brand can consistently support.
- Proof: projects, process, people, documentation, reviews, and service behavior.
- Personality: three to five traits that guide writing and design decisions.

Use a message hierarchy: promise, supporting explanation, proof, action. Every major page needs all four, but not necessarily in the same component.

## Identity system

Create named tokens instead of scattered values. Document primary and secondary colors, neutral scale, typography roles, spacing rhythm, corner treatment, icon style, image treatment, and motion behavior. Check contrast and Arabic/Latin harmony.

Use identity consistently across website, campaign creatives, social thumbnails, proposals, signage, and CRM communications. Repetition builds recognition; random visual novelty weakens it.

## Content standards

- Write specific, human headlines and remove generic superlatives.
- Connect claims to visible proof.
- Keep Arabic natural and direct; do not translate English marketing formulas literally.
- Maintain one approved spelling for company, people, places, and projects.
- Use real biographies only after names, roles, photos, and profile links are approved.
- Give Vision a destination, Mission an operating method, Values observable behaviors, and the brand promise a customer-facing commitment.

## Core company pages

Prioritize Home, Projects/Services, individual proof, About, Leadership, Contact, Privacy, and verified Reviews. Add Careers, News, Investor Relations, or Partners only when the business can maintain them.
